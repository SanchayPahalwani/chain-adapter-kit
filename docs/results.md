## Scenarios

Every scenario runs on each chain, each time on its own database schema, hot wallet and fresh
destination addresses, with worker processes (`scripts/worker-main.ts`) doing the work. Each
`kill at` scenario runs one worker at a time with that crash point switched on: every withdrawal
kills the worker there once, and the next worker carries on from what was committed.

- **kill at after_reserve**: killed after the reservation (nonce or coins) and fee are committed, before signing.
- **kill at after_sign**: killed after signing in memory, before the attempt is stored.
- **kill at after_store**: killed after the signed attempt is committed, before it is sent.
- **kill at before_broadcast**: killed after the node said it does not have the transaction, just before the send.
- **kill at after_broadcast**: killed after the node accepted the transaction, before the broadcast is recorded; the restarted worker must find each transaction on the node and record its broadcast without sending it.
- **kill at after_broadcast_recorded**: killed right after the broadcast is recorded.
- **kill at before_settle**: killed after the transaction is seen final, before the settlement is posted.
- **kill at after_settle**: killed right after the settlement is committed.
- **kill at after_replacement_store**: killed after a replacement is stored, before it is sent; blocks wait until every withdrawal has one (an attempt is stale after 1.5 s), and each withdrawal then settles on whichever of its attempts confirms, the original or a replacement.
- **random kills, 4 workers**: 4 workers at once, no crash point. Once each has run 1.5 s past start-up, withdrawals are submitted 20 at a time, each to its own fresh address, whenever fewer than 20 are unfinished, until at least 100 were submitted and at least 20 kills caught a worker holding a withdrawal (40 and 3 with --quick); then every withdrawal must settle. From the first batch on, every 150 to 600 ms the supervisor picks a random worker that has run at least 1.5 s past start-up, if there is one, SIGKILLs it and starts another. A kill caught a worker holding a withdrawal when that withdrawal's lease is still in the dead worker's name. The minimum must be reached, and everything settled and graded, within 60 s + 2 s per withdrawal of the minimum count. This run: on evm-anvil, 20 of the 28 kills caught a worker holding a withdrawal (at least 20 required); on bitcoin-regtest, 20 of the 23 kills caught a worker holding a withdrawal (at least 20 required).
- **duplicate requests**: every request is submitted 5 times at once with one idempotency key, over 5 database connections, and again after the run; 2 more are refused by policy (not_allowlisted, over_withdrawal_limit) and must stay refused.
- **dropped transaction**: no blocks until every withdrawal is broadcast; then, with the workers stopped, every attempt is dropped from the node's mempool (it held all of them, and none after); then workers and blocks resume, and each withdrawal must be sent again and settle.
- **reorg before finality**: no blocks until every withdrawal is broadcast; then one block, which the workers record at one confirmation; with the workers stopped, that block is replaced by an empty one and the node reports no attempt confirmed. A node that forgets the removed transactions (anvil) must then hold none of them, one that puts them back in its mempool (bitcoind) all of them. Workers run again, still without blocks, until each withdrawal has recorded its block as reorganised out (sending its attempt again where the node forgot it, and only there); then workers and blocks resume, and each must settle on that same attempt.
- **stuck fee**: no blocks until every withdrawal is broadcast, each first attempt accepted by the node; then, with the workers stopped, the harness raises the fee level blocks take from the next block on (how, on each chain, is in its note) and one block is mined. A node that evicts what pays less (anvil) then holds none of the first attempts, and each withdrawal must find its first attempt gone and have the re-send of it refused; a node that keeps it waiting (bitcoind) still holds all of them, and no attempt is re-sent and accepted. Then workers and blocks resume. Each withdrawal must replace its first attempt (an attempt is stale after 1.5 s); no first attempt may confirm, and each withdrawal must settle on a replacement. This run: on evm-anvil, the harness raises anvil's base fee to 100 gwei before every block, and sets it back to 1 gwei afterwards; on bitcoin-regtest, the harness mines blocks that take only transactions paying at least 3 sat/vB, 1 over the adapter's normal 2.

## What the columns mean

The balances are read from the chain, not from the pipeline's records, and only after
finality depth + 1 more blocks, once the node holds no pending transaction from the hot wallet
(on Bitcoin: no mempool transaction spends a hot-wallet coin).

- **Withdrawals**: withdrawal rows the scenario made: one per idempotency key.
- **Worker kills**: worker processes that died by SIGKILL. A `kill at` worker kills itself once per withdrawal at its crash point; in random kills the supervisor sends them, only to workers that have run for a while past start-up.
- **Settled**: withdrawals that ended `SETTLED`.
- **Rejected**: requests the policy refused (`REJECTED`); nothing may reach their destinations.
- **Sent twice**: withdrawals whose destination holds more on-chain than the amount asked, plus `REJECTED` or `FAILED` ones whose destination holds anything. Each withdrawal goes to its own fresh address, so that balance is exactly what the pipeline sent it.
- **Lost**: `SETTLED` withdrawals whose destination holds less than the amount.
- **Unfinished**: withdrawals not in a terminal state (`SETTLED`, `REJECTED`, `FAILED`) when the scenario ended.
- **Ledger drift**: the largest absolute `onchain - ledger - explained` over every asset the scenario used: what the hot wallet holds on-chain against what the ledger says it holds, less what is confirmed but not yet final. The total row shows the largest of any scenario on any chain.

A scenario passes when sent twice, lost and unfinished are 0; every valid withdrawal settled and
only the refused requests were rejected; drift is 0 and every reconciliation check passes; the node
holds no pending transaction from the hot wallet (as above); and the scenario's own check holds (its fault happened:
for example, random kills caught enough workers holding a withdrawal, and stuck fee settled every
withdrawal on a replacement).
The property: every accepted request ends in exactly one on-chain transfer or a clean rejection,
and the ledger shows no drift.
